1. What and why

A "central bank" for the IMD swarm: one contract that fees flow INTO and that registered agents can spend FROM, with the swarm's seat NFTs deciding anything large. The owner's framing, in order:

  1. "A spending pool fees could flow into, and that agents could spend from."
  2. "Make it require a quorum of votes by the swarm NFTs."
  3. "There are over 1000 active agents. Prevent the wallets that hold 20–40 seats from having control."

IMD's docs (imd.fun/docs, read 2026-10-15, about 70% of the page; the oracle section and tail were cut off) have no native pool, vault or seat-voting feature. All of this is a custom contract set, launched through IMD's own job flow.

2b. Launch-pool fee claim: how the payer's 1% actually arrives (proven on chain 2026-10-19)

Checked against one launch token, launch 1205 on Robinhood (4663), payer 0x28AA…C2DB, LaunchFactory 0xa25b02a1e93903b790e6aaf7da1b8b8d50294645 (Sourcify-verified; the mainnet LaunchFactory 0x12c6…a96f and ProjectFactory 0xff03…7120 carry the same functions).

  • Nothing arrives by itself. The 1.25% LP fee accrues inside the factory's single liquidity position. It moves only when someone calls claimFees(uint64 launchNumber) on the factory. There is no UI, no API route, no IMD schedule. one launch token was claimed once by IMD's fee wallet (2026-10-14, by hand) and once by us (2026-10-19, tx 0xb1e2…b28e); BLACKOUT (launch 1710) is claimed every 6–12 h by its own keeper 0x7a60…75b2. The docs' example OrdersALaunch ("hand it on, or take it out") is the intended pattern for a contract.
  • Permissionless. Any address may call it (simulated and sent from a wallet that is not the payer). The payout is a PUSH inside the same tx: 80% to the requester, 20% to IMD's fee wallet 0x3f25…1f22, in BOTH pool currencies. owed(account, currency) only fills when a push fails (a contract that rejects the asset); withdraw(account, currency) then pays it out. FeesClaimed, FeesPaid, FeesOwed, RequesterChanged events.
  • The pool's currency0 is whatever the launch paired with. one launch token is IMD/one launch token, so its fees are IMD + one launch token, not ETH. A Swarm Bank launch paired with ETH pays ETH + token.
  • Recipient is movable: the current requester calls setRequester(launchNumber, next) on the factory (fee rights only; continuation rights stay with the original Intake caller).
  • Scale: one launch token (liquidity $12k, volume ~$1.2k/day) has paid its requester about $22 since launch. A 1% share needs real volume.

For the bank: (1) the pool contract (or the forwarder that is the payer) needs a claimFees step in its keeper loop, or relies on anyone calling it; (2) the pool must accept native ETH and ERC-20 pushes, or the share sits in owed until withdraw; (3) index FeesPaid to show fee income on the dashboard; (4) the launch should be a hook launch with the pool as fee recipient (an existing hook launch's model, 1.5% of all volume), with the 1% requester share as the second, smaller stream.

3. The pool contract (AgentPool)

  1. Deploy as the payer. Launch with launch.open (evm_project, paired with IMD or ETH) or workflow.open; the pool contract itself calls Intake request, so it is paidBy. The 1% fee stream lands in the pool. The pool is also the only address that can continue the project.
  2. Intake. Fees may arrive as IMD or the paired asset. sweep(token): anyone can pull fee balances in. Per-token accounting. OPEN: split inbound by basis points, default 80% spendable / 20% reserve.
  3. Spender registry. Each registered agent address has: a per-epoch budget (OPEN: 10 IMD per day), an allowlist of target contracts and selectors (Intake request for buying jobs, at minimum), a hard per-call cap.
  4. Spend path. spend(target, data, amount): caller registered, target + selector allowed, epoch budget has room, decrement, call. buyJob(body) helper pays 0.5 IMD per action through Intake so agents never hold keys. Every spend emits an event.
  5. Limits. Total outflow cap per epoch (bounds a compromised agent). Guardian can pause (pause only, no spend power).
  6. Control. The pool's owner is the governance Timelock (section 4), not an EOA. Budgets, allowlist and new agents change only through it.

4. Seat quorum (Governor + Timelock + SeatVotes)

Three ways were weighed; the first is the pick.

  1. Snapshot-based seat voting (pick). If the seat NFT is ERC721Votes: OpenZeppelin Governor + GovernorVotes + GovernorVotesQuorumFraction, one seat = one vote at the proposal snapshot. If it is plain ERC721 (likely): no snapshot is possible and a seat can vote, move, vote again. Fix: SeatVotes, a lock wrapper (deposit a seat, get a checkpointed vote weight, withdraw later). Costs seat liquidity, but is sound.
  2. Pool-owned proposal flow. Two tiers: small spends inside registered budgets need no vote; large spends, budget changes, allowlist changes and new agents need a seat vote with quorum, then the timelock. Pool owner = Timelock, proposer = Governor.
  3. Oracle-signed votes. An IMD oracle panel answers "did seats approve X". Cheap, but trusts the oracle and costs 0.5 IMD per vote. Rejected for primary treasury control; kept as an optional second chamber (section 5, item 6).

OPEN parameters: quorum 10% of seats with simple majority (many seats are idle; a high quorum deadlocks the pool); voting 3 days; delay 1 day; timelock 2 days; proposal threshold 1 seat (a few, if spam appears); guardian veto that expires or is removable by vote.

Tier 3 owns every knob of tier 2, so the seats can shrink or switch off the oracle lane if IMD's key or its panels go bad. The oracle may also serve as tier 3's second chamber (section 5, item 6).

How a tier-2 proposal runs

  1. Propose on chain. proposeSpend(target, data, amount, memo) (memo bounded, say 280 chars). The contract stores the proposal and builds the question text itself from a fixed template, so the proposer cannot frame it: "AgentPool <pool> on chain <id>, proposal <n>: pay <amount> <token> to <target>, calldata keccak <hash>. Memo: <memo>. Answer true if the swarm approves this spend." It computes questionHash = keccak256(canonicalDoc) where canonicalDoc is the compact sorted-key JSON IMD hashes ({"answerType":"bool","chainId":<id>,"evidence":"panel","question":"<text>", "v":1,"window":{"hours":24}}, plus fixed definitions), stores it, and emits the full body so anyone can submit it verbatim. Must be proven byte-for-byte against IMD's canonicalisation on Sepolia (section 7, step 4); JSON string escaping of the memo is the risky part, so restrict the memo to [A-Za-z0-9 .,:;()\-].
  2. Ask. Anyone (usually the proposer) submits that exact body to oracle.request with consumer = {chainId, pool} and pays 0.5 IMD (x402 from a wallet, or on chain through Intake from the pool's own tier-1 budget: a proposal then costs the pool 0.5 IMD, bounded like any tier-1 spend). panelSize and quorum are read from the pool (OPEN: 25 and 17, two thirds; the price is the same for 5 or 100 members, and a large panel is the only sybil defence the docs give us). validForSeconds OPEN: 3 days.
  3. Submit the attestation. Anyone calls executeAttested(n, attestation, signature). Checks, copied from AdamSplitOracle and tightened: recovered signer == the pool's oracleSigner; questionHash == proposals[n].questionHash (the pin ADAM lacks); answerType == bool and answer == true; chainId == block.chainid; panelSize ≥ pool.minPanel, quorum ≥ pool.minQuorum, agreed ≥ quorum, agreed ≤ panelSize; issuedAt ≤ now, now − issuedAt ≤ MAX_AGE, expiresAt ≥ now; !used[requestId], then mark used; amount ≤ perProposalCap and the epoch's oracle-lane total + amount ≤ epochCap. A false answer, or no attestation before expiresAt, closes the proposal; it can be re-asked as a new proposal (new 0.5 IMD).
  4. Timelock. 24 h. The guardian may cancel (pause-only role). Dissenting seats can start a tier-3 vote to cancel.
  5. Execute. Anyone calls execute(n); the pool makes the call and emits Spent like any tier-1 spend.

What the lane trusts, plainly: IMD's oracle signer (one key; bounded by the caps and replaceable by tier 3), IMD's panel selection (undocumented; the large panel and the "nobody reviews their own work" rule are the only mitigations), and the answer semantics (the panel judges the template text and memo; it never sees calldata, only its hash and the decoded target/amount in the text, so the template must print the human-readable call: token, amount, recipient, and for Intake request the job body hash).

Why not oracle-only (no tier 3): the signer key would be the pool. Keep the seats as the owner of record.

5. Anti-whale: wallets holding 20–40 seats

On chain this cannot be stopped outright: 40 seats split across 40 wallets are indistinguishable from 40 holders. The aim is to make concentration costly, slow and visible. Tools, strongest first:

  1. Distinct-holder threshold. A proposal needs the seat quorum AND a minimum number of distinct voting addresses (OPEN: 100). A whale must split across 100 wallets to pass anything alone, which is visible and easy to veto. The most reliable tool: it targets the number of voters, not the weight.
  2. Per-wallet cap. weight = min(seats, CAP), OPEN: CAP 3 to 5. 20 to 40 seats count like 5.
  3. Square-root weight. weight = sqrt(seats): 4 seats = 2, 36 seats = 6. Shrinks the edge; pairs with the cap.
  4. Lock and age. Only seats locked in SeatVotes for N days before the snapshot count (OPEN: 7). A fresh transfer costs time, which blocks buy-vote-sell and makes splitting slow.
  5. Active-agent filter. Count only seats whose worker accepted work in the last N days, or has Merkle distributor claims (ERC-8004 adapter / explorer / distributor data). Idle whale seats count for nothing; a whale running 40 real workers still counts, but pays real compute. Adds an oracle dependency for "active".
  6. Two-chamber approval. Treasury changes need the seat vote AND one of: a second vote with per-wallet cap 1, an operator multisig (an existing Safe), or an IMD oracle attestation.
  7. Delegation limit. If delegation exists, cap any delegate at 3% of seats.
  8. Veto and rage-quit. Time-boxed veto for small holders, or dissenters exit their share before a large spend executes.

Pick (as answered 2026-10-15): 1 + cap 3 and sqrt (2, 3) + 7-day lock (4) + active-agent filter (5) + timelock veto.

Trade-offs: caps and sqrt punish honest operators who run many seats (pick the cap from real holder data, section 7); the active filter needs a trusted "active" source; cap, sqrt and lock are custom SeatVotes code, so adversarial review before anything else.

  • Governor, Timelock, SeatVotes and AgentPool go in ONE evm_project or workflow.open build; adversarial review runs before any frontend (the owner's checker rule).
  • Sepolia first, with a mock seat NFT (the real one cannot be snapshotted yet) and a mock Intake.

  • This app: nothing changes until the contracts exist. Later: a /swarm-pool card (balances, budgets, open proposals) reading the contracts, and a vote button through window.directSignTx. No server key: every vote and proposal is browser-signed.

10. Development flow (brainstorm with the owner, 2026-10-18; nothing started)

Constraint: one developer, one seat, enrolling the wider community. So the flow makes the community build and pay, and the owner's job is specs, running votes and the glue. Progressive decentralisation in three stages.

Stage A: signal, no contracts (weeks, cheap). Goal is enrolment, not a treasury.

  • Idea page + this spec, public (whitepaper pattern).
  • Seat-verified signal votes in THIS app: login exists (SIWE), the operator app already maps every seat to its owner. A /pool page: proposals as text, one vote per seat by signed message, live tally. Non-binding, no gas, no audit. Ships from this repo.
  • A Safe as the pool: the owner plus two to four community seat holders, 3-of-5 (OPEN: who). Fees from a launch flow to it. Spend only what a signal vote passed; publish every tx. The dashboard reads the Safe: balance, every outflow, matched proposal.
  • Founding oracle question (0.5 IMD, panel 25+): "Should the IMD swarm hold a shared, seat-governed spending pool?" The signed attestation is the social-proof artefact for the pitch and dogfoods tier 2 before any contract.

Stage B: contracts (months). Governor + Timelock + SeatVotes + AgentPool replace the Safe's signers. Sepolia with a mock seat NFT first. Signal votes become binding votes on the same page and login, only the submit path changes (directSignTx to the Governor).

Stage C: oracle lane + agents spending. Tier 1 budgets and tier 2 questions, once Stage B has run clean.

Token design: the launch IS the token. No governance token: seats govern (the anti-whale point). The pool's own IMD launch (launch.open; in Stage A the Safe pays, in Stage B the pool contract) gives: 80% in the pool and 1% of every trade back to the treasury (the fee source); 10% to the swarm via the Merkle distributor, every connected seat gets a slice: the enrolment mechanic, and the claim card already exists (the operator app): "claim your share, then come vote". The token is liquidity plus fee source, never a vote weight. No yield language (whitepaper rule).
